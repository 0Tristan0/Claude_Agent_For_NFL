A great business priced for economics no GPU cloud has shown

Nebius is the best-funded independent GPU cloud and is growing 454% a year. At $165, the stock needs long-run margins of about 70% or a cost of capital near 9%. My base case delivers neither, so I rate it Sell.

01Summary

The operating story is excellent: Q2-26 revenue of $582m (+454% YoY), $3.0bn of ARR, and more than $40bn of committed backlog with Microsoft and Meta [F]. The equity story depends on three numbers the market rarely discusses: capex per dollar of ARR, mature EBITDA margin, and how many years a GPU earns.

At about $2.70 of capex per $1 of ARR, a 60% EBITDA margin and a 6-year GPU life, each new megawatt earns an unlevered pre-tax IRR of about 8–10%. That is below an 11.3% WACC, so growth adds revenue but little value per share [I].

Three reasons for Sell

  1. The unit economics do not clear the cost of capital at base-case assumptions. The "22-month payback" for Q2 contracts and a BNP-linked "1.3-year payback" both count customer prepayments as cash inflows. Prepayments are financing, not return.
  2. Dilution and debt-like items are understated. Fully diluted shares are 343m, not the ~272m that quote screens show. There are $13.45bn of convertibles converting at $51–$325, $6.0bn of prepaid service still owed, and a base-case funding gap of about $49bn by 2029.
  3. Consensus sits near my bull case, and sector credit is cracking. Street 2030 revenue is about $45bn against my $32bn base. CoreWeave's 5-year CDS hit about 1,195bp on 15 Oct, and Gartner expects more than 80% of neoclouds to lose independence by 2030.

What would make me a buyer

  • A price below about $95, a 20% discount to fair value.
  • Disclosed capex of $2.40 or less per $1 of ARR with mature margins of 62% or more.
  • Older GPUs (H100/H200) re-contracting at 70% or more of their original rates, which would point to a 7-year-plus economic life.

Competitive position

Real advantages: deep ex-Yandex infrastructure engineering with its own server and rack designs; a near-net-cash corporate balance sheet against CoreWeave's ~$52bn of debt; customer-funded capex (Microsoft ~$6.96bn of upfront payments, $4.4bn of advance payments in H1-26 [F]); 5 GW of contracted power targeted by year-end [G]; and NVIDIA as a 7% holder with a >5 GW partnership.

Limits: GPU capacity is a largely commoditised input bought from the same supplier as everyone else. The biggest customers are also the biggest competitors; reports on Meta's own cloud plans cut the stock about 14% on 1 Jul. Training workloads move to the cheapest capacity, and the software layer is not yet a material share of revenue [I].

Management and governance

Co-founder and CEO Arkady Volozh and CFO Dado Alonso have executed exceptionally: year-end 2025 active power was ~170 MW against a 100 MW target, and ARR was $1.25bn against $0.9–1.1bn guidance [F]. Class B shares carry 10 votes, and Volozh's trust holds 28.66m of the 33.46m Class B shares, giving him roughly half of all votes [F/I]. Discounts: dual-class control, SBC at 18% of Q2 revenue, and a 4-to-5-year depreciation change that flatters EBIT.

03Industry

Demand comes from frontier-model training, fast-growing inference, enterprise and sovereign AI, and hyperscalers renting outside capacity because power is their bottleneck. NVIDIA puts its own revenue opportunity at about $25bn per GW for Blackwell and $40bn per GW for Vera Rubin, and expects neocloud capacity of about 8 GW by late 2026 [C]. Nebius's 0.8–1.0 GW of connected power would be roughly 10–12% of that [I].

2026 is a seller's market. H100 one-year rental rates rose about 40% from their Oct-25 low, Nebius's first mid-term capacity auction cleared about 15% above any prior rate, and on-demand prices rose on 1 Oct (reported at 17–21%, single source) [C/G]. I read this as a cyclical peak: prices fell sharply in 2024–25 when supply caught up, and the 2026–27 build wave (CoreWeave $35–39bn of capex, Nebius $20–25bn, IREN $25–30bn, plus Lambda and Crusoe) is still to be energised [I].

Calibration [I]: ARR per active MW was about $7.4m at year-end 2025 on a Hopper-heavy fleet. A GB300-class megawatt of IT load holds about 470 GPUs.   • Value is created only with capex of about $2.40 or less per $1 of ARR and margins of 60% or more, or with GPU lives of 7 years or more.
  • Payback figures that count prepayments as returns are misleading. A prepayment is a customer loan repaid in service: it lowers the equity needed but not the project's return. The BNP-linked figure implies about $30bn of yearly revenue per GW, roughly twice what GPU-hour arithmetic supports.
  • CoreWeave, at about $13bn of revenue, guides FY26 adjusted operating income to only $0.96–1.15bn [F]. That is the clearest real-world read of mature neocloud returns.

What $165 implies

Holding my base ARR and WACC, the price needs long-run EBITDA margins of 65% at 2.2x capex per $ of ARR, 68% at 2.4x, 71% at 2.6x, or 76% at 2.9x. CoreWeave runs at 59%. Alternatively, the price discounts my base case at about 9.1%.

12Thesis & catalysts

Bull case

  1. Power, not GPUs, limits supply through 2028, and 5 GW of contracted power is scarce. Auction and price-list increases show pricing power.
  2. Microsoft and Meta prepayments plus investment-grade debt let Nebius grow 3–4x with limited dilution; Meta's backstop protects unsold capacity.
  3. Software and inference could lift mature margins above 65% and stretch GPU lives to 7–8 years.
  4. NVIDIA owns ~7%; a hyperscaler could buy Nebius for power and talent.

Bear case

  1. GPU rental earns about its cost of capital over a cycle; CoreWeave's ~8% operating margin at $13bn revenue is the evidence.
  2. The compute base must be re-bought every 5–7 years, eating about half of mature EBITDA.
  3. The largest buyers set prices, own the demand, and build their own capacity.
  4. Growth needs ~$50bn of outside capital: equity works at $165 and becomes punitive at $80.

What the market may be missing

  • About 98% of H1-26 operating cash flow was customer prepayments, so "positive operating cash flow" overstates cash generation.
  • True diluted shares are about 26% above what quote screens show, and the convertibles add about 68m more as the stock rises.
  • Moving servers from 4- to 5-year depreciation adds about $168m to 2026 EBIT (company estimate); peers on 6 years are flattered more.
  • Meta's backstop insures unsold capacity, but part of the 2027–28 demand signal is Meta's own planning.
  • The 5 GW power portfolio has value to hyperscalers even in the bear case, so I do not model the equity at zero.
  • ClickHouse is a real asset worth about $11–20 a share, but it is not a thesis driver and it inflates GAAP earnings through revaluation gains.

13Recommendation

Sell (Underweight). Fair value $120; 12-month target $135, about 18% below $165.47. Medium conviction.

In my base case Nebius reaches about $32bn of revenue and $19bn of EBITDA in 2030, a remarkable outcome. After funding a ~$49bn gap (about 148m new shares if half is equity), each share is worth about $194 in 2030 at 5x EBITDA (≈4% a year) or $281 at 7x (≈13% a year). Weighted across scenarios, the expected return is about 2–10% a year, below a 13.7% cost of equity. The bear case loses most of the investment; the bull case compounds at about 23%.

  1. Returns on new capacity are roughly equal to the cost of capital, so growth alone does not create value per share.
  2. Dilution, prepaid obligations and the funding gap are larger than headline figures suggest.
  3. The price assumes the bull case, at a ~45% EBITDA-multiple premium to CoreWeave, while sector credit stress rises.

This is a call on valuation, not on business quality. I would revisit below about $95 or on the evidence listed above.
